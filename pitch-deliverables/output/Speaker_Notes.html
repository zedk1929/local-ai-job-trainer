Local AI Coach

LOCAL AI COACH — SPEAKING SCRIPT About 4–5 minutes, including a short demo. This is a practice pitch. Maya and Eli are fictional examples. The $15/month price is a hypothesis. No interviews, customers, revenue, time savings, or improved scores are claimed. Baseline scores come from the user's reported run/screenshots and were not rerun for this deck. Product status reflects the latest review in this conversation, not a fresh code audit.

Slide 1: Local AI Coach

Imagine you run a small café. Every week, you read customer comments and sort them into issues you can act on. You try a local AI, but you still have to check whether it understands your rules. Local AI Coach tests that model, asks a stronger AI to diagnose its mistakes, and lets you measure whether better instructions help. Today, Survey Cleaner is our working example.

Slide 2: Maya’s weekly feedback review

Meet Maya, a fictional café owner. She collects comments through a survey and wants to know what customers mention, how they feel, and which responses need a human follow-up. This is an illustrative user story, not someone we interviewed. Her difficulty is consistency. A comment can praise the coffee and criticize the staff in the same sentence. A useful assistant needs to handle both.

Slide 3: A model score is only the beginning

Getting an answer from a model is easy. Knowing whether to trust its answers is harder. Maya needs examples of mistakes, a way to adjust the instructions, and a fair comparison before adopting a change. Our product brings those steps into one workflow. It also keeps the original results so she can see when a proposed improvement makes things worse.

Slide 4: The improvement workflow

Here is the flow. Upload a CSV and map the columns. Run the original local model against human-labeled responses. With permission, send a development subset to the parent AI for analysis. Review and edit the proposed rules, then approve a local comparison. The parent does not receive the comparison responses. That separation helps us evaluate the proposal, although this is still development data rather than a final independent test.

Slide 5: One mistake, one proposed correction

Take the fictional comment from Maya's café. Suppose the model calls it positive and only labels drink quality. The parent could propose: include every explicitly mentioned topic, and use mixed sentiment when praise and criticism appear together. Maya reviews that suggestion. We then test the revised instructions on other responses. This example explains the mechanism. It is not a measured before-and-after result from our app.

Slide 6: Our original local-model results

These are the original model results reported from our fictional survey dataset. Sixty nonblank responses had human labels. Topic micro-F1 was 77.6 percent, sentiment accuracy was 71.7 percent, and review-flag accuracy was 90 percent. Schema validity was 98.3 percent. This shows the local model can do useful work and still has weaknesses to investigate. These are baseline results, not evidence that the parent improved it.

Slide 7: The demo: inspect, propose, compare

In the demo, I would open the original results and show one real recorded mistake. Then I would open the saved parent analysis and explain a proposed rule. Finally, I would show the comparison on the reserved responses, if that run is complete and verified. We do not yet have a verified improvement result to put on this slide. If a change fails, the honest outcome is to keep the original.

Slide 8: A first customer hypothesis

Our first customer hypothesis is a small team that repeatedly categorizes written feedback using its own rules. Maya represents an owner-operator who could pay. Eli, a fictional campus event organizer, represents an accessible pilot user. We would recruit through campus organizations and direct conversations with local businesses. A possible price to test is fifteen dollars a month, with parent API usage separate. We have not validated that price, demand, or savings.

Slide 9: Our next proof: a useful change

Our next milestone is one real user completing the workflow and one verified comparison showing whether a proposed change helped. The longer-term direction is an exportable local worker, connections to tools such as Google Sheets, and eventually optional adapter training. Those are future capabilities. For now, we are looking for people who repeatedly review written feedback and are willing to test whether this workflow earns their trust.
