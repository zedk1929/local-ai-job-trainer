SLIDE 4: THE CONCRETE TEST — "GLASS IN LATTE" + RULE EDITOR (Paper)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
• THE REAL-WORLD EXAMPLE: From our campus dining feedback dataset: "Found shattered glass in my iced latte. Almost swallowed it!"
• THE CATASTROPHIC FAILURE: Out of the box, the baseline local AI labeled sentiment as "unclear" and left needs_review as false!
• THE CONSEQUENCE: A life-threatening food safety hazard was buried in the unread queue.
• THE COACHING LESSON: Parent AI caught the flaw and wrote a hard rule: "Foreign objects or hazards must NEVER be unclear. Flag negative sentiment and alert review immediately."
• THE APPRENTICE RESULT: With the approved rule, Apprentice instantly classified it negative, flagged review, and triggered an emergency alert in seconds.
• POINT TO SCREENSHOT: Show judges the real Failure Analysis dashboard and in-browser rule editor where this exact rule was reviewed and locked.

JUDGE RAPID-FIRE Q&A PLAYBOOK
================================================================================
Q1: "Is this actually fine-tuning model weights or prompt steering?"
A1: "Today it is prompt and rule refinement with deterministic schema enforcement and reserved verification. That means zero GPU training cost, instant deployment, and zero catastrophic forgetting. Our Q2 roadmap adds optional on-device LoRA adapter tuning for tasks that hit prompting ceilings."

Q2: "What hardware does the user need?"
A2: "Any modern laptop with 8GB RAM can run Qwen 2.5 Coder 3B or Llama 3.2 3B smoothly via Ollama. On an Apple M-series or modest GPU, it runs at over 50 tokens per second. We also provide a simulated Preview Mode so judges can explore the full app without downloading any local models."

Q3: "How do you guarantee privacy if you use Gemini or Claude?"
A3: "Privacy is user-controlled: only a small development sample of 10 to 20 anonymized examples is sent to the parent AI during setup to synthesize the rules. Once approved, 100% of the actual customer data and recurring production inference runs fully offline on the user's computer. The reserved evaluation set and future batches never touch the cloud."

Q4: "Why pay $29 instead of just writing prompts in ChatGPT?"
A4: "Three reasons: First, 84% of surveyed Cal Poly students lack the prompt engineering expertise to achieve high F1 scores. Second, our system includes an automated evaluation harness with reserved test sets so you can mathematically verify improvement. Third, ChatGPT cannot run offline on confidential medical, financial, or student records."
